# Narrative Frameworks Reference

Detailed guide to story structures for presentations. Load this when exploring frameworks with users.

## Framework Details

### 1. Problem → Solution → Impact

**Best for:** Pitches, proposals, sales presentations, funding requests

**Structure:**
1. **Problem** (1-3 slides): What's broken, painful, or missing? Make the audience feel the problem.
2. **Solution** (2-4 slides): How do you fix it? Be specific and credible.
3. **Impact** (1-2 slides): What changes when the solution is implemented? Quantify if possible.

**Example flow:**
- "Companies waste 40% of meeting time on status updates" (problem)
- "Our async tool replaces 80% of status meetings" (solution)
- "Teams recover 6 hours per week per person" (impact)

**Tips:**
- Start with the audience's pain, not your product
- The solution should feel inevitable after the problem
- Impact should answer "so what?"

---

### 2. Situation → Complication → Resolution (SCR)

**Best for:** Strategic updates, board presentations, analysis presentations

**Structure:**
1. **Situation** (1-2 slides): Establish shared context. What's the baseline everyone agrees on?
2. **Complication** (2-3 slides): What changed? What challenge emerged? What's at stake?
3. **Resolution** (2-4 slides): What's the path forward? What decision or action is needed?

**Example flow:**
- "Q3 revenue grew 15% YoY, on track with plan" (situation)
- "But customer acquisition cost rose 40%, threatening Q4 margins" (complication)
- "We recommend shifting 30% of paid spend to organic channels" (resolution)

**Tips:**
- Situation should be uncontroversial—build agreement
- Complication creates tension that demands resolution
- Resolution should feel like the logical answer to the complication

---

### 3. What → So What → Now What

**Best for:** Data presentations, research findings, quarterly reports

**Structure:**
1. **What** (2-4 slides): Present the facts, data, or findings. Be objective.
2. **So What** (2-3 slides): Interpret the implications. Why does this matter?
3. **Now What** (1-2 slides): Recommend actions. What should we do about it?

**Example flow:**
- "NPS dropped from 45 to 32 this quarter" (what)
- "This correlates with our support response time increasing 2x" (so what)
- "We need to hire 3 more support reps by month end" (now what)

**Tips:**
- Keep "What" factual—save interpretation for "So What"
- "So What" is where you add value—don't skip it
- "Now What" should be concrete and actionable

---

### 4. Before → After → Bridge

**Best for:** Case studies, transformation stories, change management

**Structure:**
1. **Before** (2-3 slides): Paint the old state. What was the pain, limitation, or status quo?
2. **After** (2-3 slides): Show the new state. What's better, faster, easier?
3. **Bridge** (2-4 slides): Explain the transformation. How did you get from before to after?

**Example flow:**
- "Manual invoice processing took 3 days and 4 people" (before)
- "Automated processing takes 10 minutes with zero errors" (after)
- "We implemented AI extraction + approval workflows in 6 weeks" (bridge)

**Tips:**
- Make "Before" vivid—the more painful, the better
- "After" should be aspirational but believable
- "Bridge" builds credibility—show the work

---

### 5. Hook → Build → Payoff

**Best for:** Keynotes, storytelling, persuasive presentations

**Structure:**
1. **Hook** (1-2 slides): Grab attention immediately. Surprise, provoke, or intrigue.
2. **Build** (4-8 slides): Develop your argument. Layer evidence, stories, and logic.
3. **Payoff** (1-2 slides): Deliver the conclusion. Reward the audience's attention.

**Example flow:**
- "What if I told you 90% of startups fail because of a problem you can fix in one meeting?" (hook)
- "[Series of evidence, examples, and frameworks about founder alignment]" (build)
- "The one meeting: a structured co-founder expectations conversation before you incorporate" (payoff)

**Tips:**
- Hook should create a question in the audience's mind
- Build should create mounting anticipation
- Payoff should feel earned, not obvious

---

## Choosing the Right Framework

| If the goal is... | Consider... |
|-------------------|-------------|
| Get funding or approval | Problem → Solution → Impact |
| Update leadership on progress | Situation → Complication → Resolution |
| Present research or data | What → So What → Now What |
| Tell a transformation story | Before → After → Bridge |
| Persuade or inspire | Hook → Build → Payoff |

## Hybrid Approaches

Frameworks can be combined:
- **SCR + PSI**: Use SCR for context, then PSI for the recommendation
- **Hook + What/So What/Now What**: Open with a hook, then present data
- **Before/After + PSI**: Show transformation, then pitch scaling the solution
